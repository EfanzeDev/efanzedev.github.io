On Commercial Age Ratings

If you look on the box or store page of a video game, you're likely to see an Age Rating. E for Everyone, G for General, or maybe your country just uses a number. Either way, there has to be a way for a game to signify its target age demographic to legally be sold in all major countries.

For indie games, made by individuals or small teams, there have been several attempts. The Independednt Game Rating System (Or TIGRS) was an early option, taken offline before being superseded by the Open Source Game Rating System (OGRS), which is still available for use today, but ceased active support in 2019. Both of these systems had flaws, but were the best options for something familiar and easy to understand.

The biggest issue however is that age rating systems like these need to be known, trusted and widely used. TIGRS and OGRS were not. And indie developers didn't feel the need to have their games rated by the major classification boards as the cost was too great to justify something unnecessary for independent distribution.

Developers took to the humble content warning, which did everything an age rating did, except people actually took notice of it. Indie games have been a safe space for all since. But in my opinion, the space could use something that, at a glance, would help you decide whether or not a game is right for you.

My Age Rating System

I've put together a four-step age rating system as a result. These do not replace traditional content warnings, they are designed to compliment them.

6+

For games generally acceptable for all ages.

10+

For games that contain scenes or content that would frighten young children.

14+

For games that include use of profanity, references to controlled substances or moderate violence.

18+

For games that allow the player to engage with porn, poker, party pills or (excessive) profanity.

The Role of the Ratings

So, if I plan on using content warnings anyway, why separate my games into age brackets? The rating acts as a first sign, visible where you don't need to see any content from the game or its store page, that a game is right for you.

I plan on putting these age ratings in the thumbnails of my games once I perfect the system and it becomes recognisable. That way, the user will always know immediately if a game isn't right for them. If they are within the age bracket, content warnings will provide further details if applicable.

The system is already in use this way on my website, and I've recently added it as text to the short descriptions of my Itch.io games, visible across the site.

I'm aware that this is not a perfect solution. Games are still not rated by an objective third-party, the graphics are not recognisable or known, and the only person that knows how it works is me. This is why this site is not an advertisement or guide to use the system, it's to spread awareness and clarify its purpose to you, the end user.

My age ratings are designed by me to suit my needs. I do not currently recommend that you adopt my age rating system. If you do, all responsibility for incorrectly rated games, missing content warnings and more will fall squarely on you.

What I can recommend is to look into what other developers are doing to ensure their games reach the correct audience, and if you're interested in my work, to keep an eye out for the age bracket rating system as I continue to work on it.
